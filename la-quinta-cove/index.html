Water Damage Restoration in The Cove, La Quinta, CA

The Cove is an older La Quinta neighborhood of 1930s casitas and later homes. Here is how water damage plays out there, and what to do first.

Call (760) 769-1159

Where The Cove is and what it is

The Cove, historically called Vale La Quinta, sits at the foot of the Santa Rosa Mountains. A City of La Quinta planning presentation describes its edges as Calle Tampico to the north, Avenida Bermudas to the east, Calle Tecate to the south, and Avenida Montezuma and Bear Creek to the west.

It matters for water damage because The Cove is not one kind of house. The city's Historic Resource Survey, completed in April 2023, shows residential building here from the 1930s through the 1970s, with new housing added in the early 1980s. A 1930s adobe casita and a later stucco house can sit on the same street.

What the city's records say about Cove housing

According to the City of La Quinta, the Cove was subdivided between 1933 and 1937, and the city's historic sites guide says more than 50 of the original adobe casitas still stand, most of them private homes.

We do not claim older homes leak more. What the records support is that materials differ from house to house. Thick adobe walls, older plaster, clay tile roofs and later additions all dry and repair differently, which is why a moisture reading matters more than the age of the house.

Water damage situations we see in a neighborhood like this

Most calls start inside the house, not outside. A failed supply line, a water heater, a toilet or sink leak, a washer hose or an air conditioner drain overflow can wet floors and walls in any home.

Storms are a smaller but real part of the picture. The city's storm safety page records that during Tropical Storm Hilary in August 2023 it handed out roughly 80,000 sandbags and 400 tons of sand by the evening of August 19. That is a citywide figure, and it does not tell us which streets took water. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Check an address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

First-hour safety steps

Stop the source if you can do it safely. Close the shutoff valve at the fixture, or the main water valve for the house. Stay out of standing water if outlets, cords or appliances are in it, and do not switch breakers while standing in water. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and contact your utility or emergency services first.

Lift valuables and rugs off wet floors and keep children and pets away from water that came from a drain or toilet. Take photos of the damage and the source before you move things. Do not run an ordinary household vacuum on wet floors.

How extraction and drying work in an older home

A crew removes standing water with extraction units, then maps moisture with meters and a thermal camera. Wet carpet pad, insulation and soaked drywall or plaster are removed when they cannot dry in place, and air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach the drying goal. Thick masonry walls dry slowly, so readings decide when equipment leaves.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for this kind of work. If the home is one of the historic casitas, ask the City of La Quinta Design and Development Department whether a repair to the building needs review before you start rebuilding.

Insurance documentation

Whether a loss is covered depends on the cause and on your policy, so check with your insurer. Photos, a note of when you found the water, moisture readings and an itemized scope can help describe the damage and the work. None of them secures coverage. The California Department of Insurance notes that standard homeowners policies do not cover flood.

Do older adobe casitas dry differently?

Thick adobe and plaster can hold moisture longer than a thin modern wall. That is why drying is decided by meter readings.

Can I use fans and a vacuum myself?

Fans help a small clean spill. For anything that soaked into walls or floors, or for water from a drain or toilet, a crew with meters is the safer route.

Will my insurance pay for the cleanup?

That depends on the cause of the water and your policy terms. Ask your insurer, and keep your photos and records.
