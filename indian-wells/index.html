Gated golf communities and the water problems that come with them

Indian Wells is a small city of resort and gated golf communities, and that shapes almost every call. Large homes have long plumbing runs, several bathrooms and often a second AC system. A leak behind a wall can travel a long way before anyone sees it, and a night emergency is no different.

Those homes also tend to be furnished with stone, hardwood and fine rugs, so hours matter. A water mitigation crew extracts and dries first, then protects what can be saved. Our dispatch line, (760) 769-1159, is answered 24/7, including overnight.

Condo or single-family: who handles what

Ownership rules decide the first phone call. In a single-family home inside a gated country club, the loss is usually yours to manage, though the HOA may have rules about contractors, hours and vehicle access. In a condo, the association often carries insurance on the building and shared pipes while you carry coverage for the interior and belongings. Ask any company you call whether its crews follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

Read your HOA documents and policy before an emergency, not during one, and keep the dispatch line saved. If water starts in a unit above, photograph the ceiling and walls, notify the association, and ask your insurer what to do. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, gradual leaks often are not, and flood damage is usually excluded.

Older systems inside newer-looking homes

A house can look fresh after a remodel and still have a 25-year-old water heater, original shutoff valves and aging supply lines behind the walls. The U.S. Tank heaters are generally built for roughly 10 to 15 years of service, per the U.S. Department of Energy. A tank beyond that age in a laundry closet or garage deserves replacement before it fails at night.

Many owners are away for part of the year. A leak in a vacant villa can run for days, so install a leak sensor, know your main shutoff, and ask a neighbor or property manager to check in.

After the water is out: repairs and permits

Once readings show the structure is dry, water damage repair replaces drywall, trim, cabinets and flooring. HOAs often control paint colors, flooring underlayment in upper units and contractor schedules, so confirm approvals before demolition starts. You hear the scope and the pricing method before any work begins.

Work that alters or repairs a structure generally needs a permit, so ask the City of Indian Wells before rebuilding walls, wiring or plumbing. Between the two, the permit and the HOA approval, get the paperwork in order before the rebuild, and save every receipt for your insurer.

If water is still spreading in Indian Wells

Before the crew reaches your Indian Wells address, work through these five steps.

Alert the gate and the HOA

Tell the guard house or management office a restoration crew is expected so the vehicle is not turned away at the entry.

Know who owns the pipe

In a condo, the leak may start in a neighbor's unit or a shared riser. Note the location and tell the association right away.

Shut the nearest valve

Use the fixture valve first, then the unit or house main. Open a faucet lower in the home to drain the line.

Power down wet areas safely

In an emergency, only touch the panel if the floor around it is dry. If water reaches outlets or a gas smell appears, call the utility or 911 first.

Protect finishes and furniture

Lift rugs and art, put foil or plastic under furniture legs, and photograph stone, wood and walls before anything is moved.
