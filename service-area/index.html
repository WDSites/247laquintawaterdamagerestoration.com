Utilities and agencies worth knowing before an emergency

Knowing who runs your water, sewer and power saves minutes when something fails. Indio homes are served by the Indio Water Authority for water and the Valley Sanitary District for sewer, and Coachella's water comes from the Coachella Water Authority, which the City of Coachella manages. Rancho Mirage has its own electric provider, Rancho Mirage Community Energy.

Across much of the rest of the valley, the Coachella Valley Water District is the water and sanitation provider, though some addresses are served by other agencies such as the Desert Water Agency or the Mission Springs Water District. Read your water bill to confirm which one applies to you. For a gas smell or a downed line, call the gas or electric utility or 911 first, then call us.

Storm season across the valley

Heavy desert storms are rare, but the valley is organized around them. The Coachella Valley Water District reports that its stormwater protection area includes 16 stormwater channels totaling 134 miles of regional flood protection facilities, which exist because runoff here moves fast across hard ground.

Before Tropical Storm Hilary in August 2023, Riverside County Emergency Management urged residents never to walk or drive through a flooded road and noted that as little as one inch of moving water can move a car.

Sandbags were handed out at city halls, corporate yards and fire stations across the valley, including La Quinta City Hall on Calle Tampico. When a storm is forecast, ask your city where to collect sandbags, and check the FEMA flood map for your address.

Safe steps while you wait for a crew

These steps apply in every town we serve, and they come from standard restoration practice and Riverside County storm guidance.

  1. Shut off the main water valve if you can reach it.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and safe to reach, and call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk.
  3. Keep people and pets out of contaminated water.
  4. Lift valuables and small furniture off wet floors.
  5. Take photos and video before anything is moved, then call the 24/7 line.

How calls differ from town to town

Indio is the valley's largest city by population, so it produces the widest mix of calls, from slab leaks to storm runoff. Indian Wells is a small city of resort and gated golf communities, where an HOA often decides who is responsible for a wall or a pipe. Palm Desert, with El Paseo and College of the Desert, mixes older condos with newer homes along the base of the mountains.

Rancho Mirage adds country club communities and a separate electric provider, and Coachella is a distinct city from both the wider valley and the festival held in Indio. Our town pages go into each of these differences, because the first useful question on a call often depends on where the house is.

Places we mention but do not list as towns

The Santa Rosa Mountains frame the west and south of La Quinta, and the wider Coachella Valley runs from the mountains past Indio to the northwest of the valley. They are landmarks for orientation, not separate service pages. If your home sits near them, the same dispatch line and the same steps apply.
